This guide starts a run of an agent or a flow for each new mail in a Gmail
or Outlook mailbox, such as your shop's support address, and shows what
each mail did.

A **mail trigger** watches one mailbox: its inbox, or one folder or Gmail
label in it. Each new mail starts a run, with the mail and the rest of its
thread as the run's input. Its runs act as the trigger's owner, with the
owner's connections. They land in the trigger's project, and each names
the trigger and its fire, one for each mail (runtime 0.2.0 or later).

Mail is text from anyone who can write to the mailbox. So in a run a mail
started, nothing changes and no more of the mailbox is read without a
person's yes, unless a project admin lists the tool
([What waits for a yes](#what-waits-for-a-yes)).

A trigger reads one account's own mailbox, a person's or the company's.
Shared mailboxes, such as a Microsoft 365 shared mailbox or a Google
group, aren't supported yet.

## Before you start

- **A connection to the mailbox:** your own Google or Microsoft account,
  or the company's mailbox, which a tenant admin connects
  ([The company's mailbox](#the-companys-mailbox)). Gmail needs
  `gmail.readonly` and Outlook `Mail.Read`, and an agent that answers needs
  `gmail.send` or `Mail.Send` too
  ([Google](../../connections/google/), [Microsoft](../../connections/microsoft/)).
- **An agent or a flow to run,** registered on your runtime. This guide
  runs the agent `acme.support-desk`.
- **Kindgi 0.2's CLI**, in your pack's directory.
- **`write` on the trigger's project, for your own mailbox, or `admin` for
  the company's,** and `execute` on the agent or flow, when authorization
  is on.

## Create one

The trigger says which mailbox to watch and what to run for each new mail
in it:

```sh
pnpm exec kindgi mail-triggers create --agent=acme.support-desk --mailbox=google --label="support inbox"
```

- **What it runs:** `--agent` runs the agent's live version, or else its
  latest. `--agent-version` pins one. `--flow` runs a flow, and needs
  `--flow-version`. There's no `--input`: each run's input is the mail
  that started it.
- **Whose mailbox:** `--mailbox=google` is Gmail and `--mailbox=microsoft`
  is Outlook. `--as=person`, the default, is your own mailbox, through
  your connection. `--as=company` is the company's, through the company's
  connection, and takes `admin` on the project
  ([The company's mailbox](#the-companys-mailbox)).
- **Which mail:** `--folder` is the Gmail label's id, or the Outlook
  folder's id or well-known name, such as `archive`. Without it, the
  trigger watches the inbox.
- **How often:** `--poll` is how often it looks for new mail, in seconds,
  30 to 3600. The default is 60.
- **How many:** `--runs-per-hour` is the most runs its mail starts in an
  hour, 1 to 600. The default is 60. A mail past it is refused, and never
  runs: staff handle it in the mailbox
  ([See what each mail did](#see-what-each-mail-did)).
- **Where:** `--project=<project-id>` is its project. Without it, it's the
  tenant's Default project.
- **`--label`** names it, for lists and the console.
- **`--allow-unattended=<tool-id>`** names a tool whose calls in its runs
  needn't wait for a person's yes, once for each tool. It takes `admin` on
  the project ([List a tool](#list-a-tool)).

It prints the trigger, with its `id`. Its first look at the mailbox only
marks where to start: mail already there doesn't run, and each mail that
comes after it does.

From your app, the clients have the same calls on `mailTriggers`:
`register`, `list`, `get`, `update`, `pause`, `resume`, `fires`,
`takeOwnership` and `unregister` (`mail_triggers` and `take_ownership` in
Python). The request's `config` holds `mailbox`, `{ app, as }`, and
`folder`, `pollSeconds` and `runsPerHour`.

## What a run gets

**An agent** gets the thread as one message: its mail oldest first, the
new mail last. The agent reads it as data, not instructions. A turn that
starts a conversation looks like this:

```text
Mail thread "Where is my order?": 3 mails, 3 shown, oldest first
--- customer@example.com, 2026-11-02 09:14 UTC
Hi, I ordered a burr grinder last week, order 1042. Has it shipped?
--- support@acme.example (this mailbox), 2026-11-02 11:40 UTC
It ships tomorrow, and you'll get its tracking number by mail.
--- NEW, id 19a4c2e7f1b3d508: customer@example.com, 2026-11-05 08:02 UTC
To: support@acme.example
No tracking number yet. Can you check?

[earlier mail left out]
Attachments: receipt.pdf (application/pdf, 84 KB)
```

- **Each mail says who it's from and when.** The new mail has its id, so
  the agent can name it, and its `To`, `Cc` and `Reply-To`. Mail the
  mailbox sent shows as "(this mailbox)". Other mail from the mailbox's
  address shows as "(this mailbox's address, not its sent mail)", since
  anyone can put that address on a mail.
- **Quoted earlier mail is left out,** since the thread already has it.
  On Gmail, it's marked `[earlier mail left out]`. What's left out is a
  run of `>` lines to the end of the mail, and an English `On … wrote:`
  line right above it; and an Outlook block that starts
  `-----Original Message-----`, or with `From:`, `Sent:` or `Date:`, and
  `Subject:` lines, when every line under it is in an earlier mail. So a
  quote in another language's form loses its `>` lines but keeps the line
  above them, such as `Le … a écrit :`, and in an answer typed between
  quoted lines, only the `>` lines after the last answer go. A forward,
  and a mail that would be left empty, keep their text whole. On Outlook,
  the turn takes only the part of each mail that Outlook marks as new,
  with no marker.
- **Links lose their tracking parameters,** such as `utm_source` and
  `fbclid`. A web address still over 100 characters keeps its scheme, its
  host and the start of its path, with `…`, so the agent can't give it back
  whole. Ready-made read tools keep every address whole.
- **Attachments are listed,** with their names, types and sizes. The agent
  doesn't read what's in them.
- **A long thread is cut to fit:** each mail's text to 50,000 characters,
  the turn to 60,000, and at most 100 mails. The turn takes the new mail,
  then the thread's first mail, then the newest, and says how many it left
  out, as in `--- 3 mails left out ---`. Each list of `To`, `Cc`, `Reply-To`
  and attachments shows its first 20, then how many more.

**A flow** gets the mail as its input: `{ mail, thread, leftOut }`.
`mail` is the new mail as the agent's turn has it, a `MailMessage`
(`@kindgi/connections/mail`): its own text, with quoted earlier mail left
out and links shortened as above, and cut to fit the turn (`bodyCut` says
so). `thread` is its thread's earlier mail, oldest first, within the
same limits, and `leftOut` counts the mails that didn't fit.

### One conversation for each thread

The mail of one thread runs one at a time, in the order the trigger found
it. A mail whose thread has a run still going waits for it: its fire is
`pending`, its note starting `thread-busy:`. A run that waits for a
person's yes holds its thread's next mail too.

For an agent, a thread is one
[conversation](../../agents/conversations/). The thread's first mail
starts it, and each later mail's turn goes into it, while it's open, in
the trigger's project, with the trigger's owner and the same agent, at
the version the trigger names, if it names one. Otherwise, such as once
the conversation is closed, the next mail starts a new conversation with
the whole thread. A closed conversation is never reopened.

- **A later turn carries only what the conversation lacks:** the new
  mail, and any earlier mail of the thread no turn gave it, such as mail
  over the hourly limit.
- **Anyone who writes into the thread later,** such as someone in `Cc`, or
  someone the thread was forwarded to, writes into the same conversation.
  It holds what the agent looked up for whoever wrote first. Have the agent
  check who wrote each mail before it gives out an order's details.

## What waits for a yes

In a run a mail started, these calls wait for a person's yes:

- **Every call of a tool that changes something:** one that isn't marked
  `mutating: false`.
- **Every call that uses a connection of the run's own mailbox,** read-only
  or not. The run already has its mail and thread, so a further read of
  that mailbox would be steered by the mail. It goes by the mailbox's app
  and whose it is: in a run on the company's Gmail, a read of the company's
  Google Calendar waits too.
- **What waits in any run no person started:** a tool that declares
  `needsApprovalWhenUnattended`, and one that uses a person's connection,
  reads included
  ([When no person started the run](../../approvals/ask-before-a-tool-runs/#when-no-person-started-the-run)).

A read that only reaches your own systems, such as a tool that finds an
order in your store and says `mutating: false`, runs unasked. A call that
uses a person's connection goes to that person to decide, and any other to
its reviewers, as in any run. In the console, its approval shows the mail
its run started from
([Deciding in the console](../../approvals/approvals-reference/#deciding-in-the-console)).

**A flow's tool step can't wait,** so a step whose tool would wait fails,
with `needs-a-person`, and the tool never runs. The run's `failureMessage`
says why in parentheses, for these two:

```text
a mail started the run, and it changes things: it isn't `mutating: false`
a mail started the run, and it uses a connection of the run's own mailbox
```

It ends:

```text
Or a project admin can list it in the mail trigger's toolsAllowedUnattended, so its runs call it without one.
```

Call such a tool from an agent step instead, where its call waits for a
person ([In a flow](../../approvals/ask-before-a-tool-runs/#in-a-flow)).

**Keep tools that reach an address the mail can name out of a
mail-started agent,** such as a fetch tool with open network access, or
don't mark them `mutating: false`. Such a read runs unasked, and can carry
what the run has read to wherever the mail points it.

### List a tool

A project admin can list tools the trigger's runs call without waiting
for a person's yes, its tools allowed unattended:

```sh
pnpm exec kindgi mail-triggers update <trigger-id> --allow-unattended=acme-shop.order-status
```

- **A stranger's mail can steer a listed tool.** Anyone who can write to
  the mailbox writes those runs' input. List a tool only if it's safe for
  any sender to make the agent call it, and keep refunds and changes to
  your store off the list.
- **It lifts only the wait for a person.** Your tenant's
  [approval rules](../../approvals/approval-rules-for-every-agent/) and the
  agent's own `hitl` still apply to a listed tool, so you can list a tool
  and keep an agent rule that asks before it runs. The call is still
  checked against the owner's access.
- **It works as a schedule's list does:** tools the agent or flow calls,
  by id, with no wildcard, at most 100. On `update` it replaces the list,
  `none` clears it, and leaving it out keeps it. A new `--agent`, `--flow`
  or mailbox must name it again, or `none`
  ([Tools allowed unattended](../../approvals/ask-before-a-tool-runs/#tools-allowed-unattended)).

## Answer in the thread

An agent answers a mail in its thread with `gmailReply` or
`outlookReply`, from `@kindgi/connections/google/tools` and
`@kindgi/connections/microsoft/tools`
([Ready-made tools](../../connections/use-a-connection/#ready-made-tools)).
It takes `{ inReplyTo, to, body }`: the mail's id, which the agent's turn
gives on the new mail's line, the address it answers, and plain text.
Give a mail-started agent the reply tool, not the send tool: a send goes
to any address it's given, so never list one to answer mail.

- **A reply goes only where the mail asks:** its Reply-To, else its From,
  as a mail client does, and `to` must name that address, in any letter
  case. A Reply-To that names no address, such as an empty group, counts
  as none. A reply is refused, and nothing is sent, when the header it
  goes by names more than one address, or the From none, when that
  address isn't one a mail tool takes, when it's this mailbox's own, or
  when `to` names another. That last refusal names the address the mail
  asks for.
- **The sender chooses both headers.** A From or a Reply-To doesn't show
  who wrote the mail, so have the agent check who's asking before it gives
  out an order's details, not by the address alone. A listed reply sends
  the agent's answer, unasked, to whatever address a stranger's mail names
  as its Reply-To.
- **It waits for a person's yes,** as a send does, unless a project admin
  lists it ([List a tool](#list-a-tool)).
- **On the company's mailbox,** the reply tool takes `as: 'company'`,
  under its own id, as in
  `gmailReply({ id: 'acme.store-mail.reply', as: 'company' })`.
- **The thread's next mail:** on Gmail, a reply carries its run's mark,
  so the conversation's next turn leaves it out, since the conversation
  has it already. An Outlook reply carries no mark, so the next turn gives
  the agent its own reply again, as mail from this mailbox.

Gmail sends the reply with `Re: ` before the subject, unless it starts
with that, and answers its id and its thread's. Outlook keeps it in the
mailbox's Sent Items, and answers `{ accepted: true }`.

## Shared or personal

A trigger on a person's mailbox reads its owner's own mail, so a person
owns it, and it's **personal**: its runs act as them, with their
connections. A trigger on the company's mailbox that a service account
owns, the project's own or another, is **shared**: its runs act as the
account. No change moves the owner: only a take-over does.

- **A personal trigger is its owner's.** Only they or a project admin can
  change, pause, resume or unregister it. Anyone else with `write` on the
  project is answered `403 permission-denied`, such as "This mail trigger
  is personal: only Sam Lee or a project admin can change it."
- **Only its owner chooses what it reads:** which of their mailboxes, and
  which folder or Gmail label in it. A project admin may change what a
  personal trigger runs and its pace, never what it reads from the owner's
  mailbox, and it keeps its owner. Anyone else is answered
  `403 permission-denied`, "Only the trigger's owner may choose which of
  their mailboxes it reads, and which folder or Gmail label in it: take it
  over to watch your own." A project admin may still move it to the
  company's mailbox.
- **A trigger on the company's mailbox takes a project admin** to create,
  "Watching the company's mailbox takes a project admin.", and to change,
  before the change or after: "Changing a trigger on the company's mailbox
  takes a project admin." A shared trigger's change does
  too, "Changing a shared mail trigger takes a project admin.", and its
  pause, resume and unregister take `write` on the project.
- **A project admin's change to a personal trigger is recorded** as
  evidence, `trigger-changed-for-owner`, with who did it, the fields
  changed, and the target, mailbox and folder before and after. Tenant
  admins can read it. The owner isn't told yet: a later runtime emails
  them. An app built on `@kindgi/api` can tell them itself, with
  `createApp`'s `triggerOwnerNotices`, as for
  [schedules](../run-on-a-schedule/#shared-or-personal).
- **Only a take-over moves the owner.** `kindgi mail-triggers
  take-ownership <trigger-id>` needs `admin` on the project: "Taking a mail
  trigger over takes a project admin." On a person's mailbox, the trigger
  then reads the new owner's own mailbox, and starts afresh there. On the
  company's, `--run-as=project` hands it to the project's own account
  instead, and `--run-as=<service-account-id>` to another service account.
- **The owner must be able to run what the trigger runs,** with an
  authorization store. A service account named with
  `--run-as=<service-account-id>`, at create or take-over, that can't run
  the agent or flow is refused (`409 trigger-owner-cannot-run-target`):
  give it the editor role on the target's project, or name another. So is
  `--run-as=project` on one the project's editors can't run: run the
  trigger as a service account that can, or own it yourself. And since no
  change moves the owner, a new agent or flow must be one the owner can
  run, a person or an account. That isn't checked when you're the owner,
  nor for an owner that was unregistered or removed. A tenant admin can
  give an account the role (not the project's own account, whose role is
  fixed), or a project admin can take the trigger over first.
- **Without an authorization store,** a project admin here is a tenant
  admin ([Without authorization](../../../deploy/people-and-keys/#without-authorization)).

`kindgi mail-triggers list --table` shows each trigger's `RUNS AS`: its
owner's name and whether it's personal or shared, such as "Sam Lee
(personal)" or "this project (shared)", or "no owner (an admin can take
it over)".

## The company's mailbox

A shop's support address is usually the company's mailbox, not a
person's. A tenant admin connects it once, signing in at Google or
Microsoft as that mailbox, such as `support@acme.example`, not as
themselves: in a project, **Connections → Connect an app**, pick the app,
and under **For** choose **The company**.

- **Allow it only in the projects that need it.** The company's
  connection serves every project its app's registration allows, and in
  those projects, anyone who may run an agent or a flow can have it read
  the company's mail. Register the app for those projects only
  (`--projects` on `kindgi connection-apps add`), which limits people's
  own connections of it to them too.
- **Have the project own the trigger,** so it doesn't depend on the admin
  who made it:

  ```sh
  pnpm exec kindgi mail-triggers create --agent=acme.support-desk --mailbox=google --as=company \
    --run-as=project --label="support inbox"
  ```

  Its runs act as the project's own account, made the first time. To have
  another service account own it, name it by id instead
  (`--run-as=<service-account-id>`), which needs `act_as` on it.
  `--run-as` goes with `--as=company` only. Without it, the trigger is
  personal to the admin who made it.
- **A tool that uses the company's mailbox** is the ready-made tool with
  `as: 'company'`, under its own tool id
  ([Ready-made tools](../../connections/use-a-connection/#ready-made-tools)).

## See what each mail did

```sh
pnpm exec kindgi mail-triggers fires <trigger-id> --table
```

Each new mail is one **fire**. The list is newest first, with when it
fired, its outcome, the run it started, and a note. A fire is kept for 30
days, and longer while its run isn't finished, such as one waiting for a
person's yes:

- **`started`:** a run started for the mail.
- **`pending`:** it waits for an earlier mail of its thread
  (`thread-busy:`).
- **`skipped`:** it wasn't for a run. The note says why, such as: the
  mailbox's own address sent it (`own-address:`), it's an automatic reply
  (`auto-submitted:`), it came before the trigger began watching
  (`before-since:`), it was gone before it was read (`not-found:`), the
  connection now reaches another account (`mailbox-changed:`), or an
  earlier turn of its thread already gave it to the agent
  (`mail-already-given:`). A `cursor-reset:` row says where the trigger
  started looking afresh, and why.
- **`refused`:** the note says why, such as: it's over the hourly limit
  (`over-limit:`), the mailbox's connection was missing
  (`connection-missing:`), the app gave it no thread (`no-thread-id:`),
  too many of the tenant's runs were waiting (`rate-limited:`), the
  database was busy (`database-busy:`), the owner may no longer run the
  agent or flow, or the trigger was paused or removed before the run
  started. **Such a mail never runs,** then or later: staff handle it in
  the mailbox. A later mail of its thread still brings it to the agent, in
  that mail's turn.
- **`failed`:** its run didn't start, or the app failed it after Kindgi's
  tries. The note says why.

## When it stops reading

A trigger's `statusReason` says why it isn't reading mail:

- **`connection-missing`:** the mailbox's connection is gone or needs
  reconnecting. The trigger keeps looking, and once its owner, or a tenant
  admin for the company's mailbox, connects again, it reads on from where
  it stopped, so mail that came meanwhile runs then. A mail it had already
  found when the connection went is `refused`, and never runs.
- **`owner-refused`:** its owner was removed from the tenant, it's a
  service account that's no longer registered, or the owner may no longer
  run its agent or flow. Nothing more is read, and its looks don't pause
  it, but mail it had already found is `refused` when its run would
  start, and those fires count toward the pause. Restore the owner's
  access, have a project admin take it over (a person's mailbox then
  starts afresh), or unregister it. The next look that opens the mailbox
  clears it.
- **Paused:** after 5 fires in a row that were refused or failed, the
  trigger pauses itself, with the last reason. A refusal for the hourly
  limit, a missing connection, a missing thread or a busy runtime doesn't
  count, nor does a run that didn't start in time, nor a skip. When the
  app is down, a look that fails only moves the next look.

When the app no longer knows where the trigger left off, such as after the
runtime was down for long, the trigger catches up from its last good
look: at most the newest 100 mails that came since, and staff handle the
rest in the mailbox. A `cursor-reset:` row in its fires says so.

## Pause it, change it

- `kindgi mail-triggers pause <trigger-id>` stops it looking for new mail.
  `resume` starts it afresh: mail that came while it was paused doesn't
  run.
- `kindgi mail-triggers update <trigger-id>` changes what it runs, its
  mailbox, folder, pace or label, with `create`'s flags but `--project`
  and `--run-as`.
  `--mailbox` and `--as` go together, since the mailbox is replaced whole.
  A new mailbox or folder starts afresh too. Without `--folder`, it keeps
  its folder, unless the mailbox changes: then it watches the new
  mailbox's inbox. Its owner stays as it is
  ([Shared or personal](#shared-or-personal)).
- `kindgi mail-triggers unregister <trigger-id>` removes it.

## Its versions

Each change to a mail trigger makes a **version** of it, which never
changes after (runtime 0.3.0 or later). Creating it makes version 1. An
`update` that changes something makes the next, and so does a take-over to
another owner. An `update` that changes nothing makes none, nor do pausing,
resuming or removing it. A trigger from before versions has none until its
next change or its next look at the mailbox.

A mail's run uses the newest version when it starts, and keeps it whatever
later changes make: the run's `trigger.version` names it. So a change or a
take-over reaches the next run that starts, never a run already going.

From your app, newest first:

- `GET /v1/mail-triggers/{triggerId}/versions` pages them
  (`mailTriggers.versions` in TypeScript, `mail_triggers.list_versions` in
  Python);
- `GET /v1/mail-triggers/{triggerId}/versions/{version}` reads one
  (`getVersion`, `get_version`). A version the trigger doesn't have is
  `404 trigger-version-not-found`.

From the CLI:

```sh
pnpm exec kindgi mail-triggers versions <trigger-id> --table
pnpm exec kindgi mail-triggers get <trigger-id> <version>
```

- `versions` pages them, with `--limit` and `--cursor`. Its table shows
  each one's number, when it was made, by whom, and why. `Kindgi` stands
  in the `BY` column for a version the runtime made itself.
- `get <trigger-id> <version>` reads one. Without a version, `get` is the
  trigger as it is now, and its `version` is the newest. The `VERSION`
  column of `list --table` shows it, empty for a trigger that has none
  yet.

Whoever can read the trigger can read its versions. Each one says when it
was made (`createdAt`), by whom (`createdBy`), and why (`cause`:
`register`, `edit` or `owner` for a take-over to another owner). Two
causes name no one, since the runtime made the version itself: `first-fire`,
for a trigger from before versions; and `unversioned-edit`, at its next look
at the mailbox or just before an edit or take-over, after a release from
before versions changed the trigger during an upgrade. Its `definition` is
what it ran, as whom, which mailbox and folder it read, at what pace, its
label and its tools allowed unattended: never its status or where it is in
the mailbox.

The evidence of a change names the version it made (`version`), on
`trigger-changed-for-owner`, `trigger-owner-set` and
`trigger-tools-allowed-unattended-set`, and that version has what the
entry leaves out.

## In the console

A project's **Mail triggers** page, under **Run**, lists its mail
triggers. Each row has:

- its label, and what it watches, such as "The company's Gmail, Inbox";
- whether it's **Active** or **Paused**, and, when it isn't reading mail,
  why: **Not connected**, **Owner refused** or **Paused by the runtime**
  ([When it stops reading](#when-it-stops-reading));
- what it starts, such as "the agent Support Desk" or "the flow Refunds
  1.0.0";
- whether it's **Shared** or **Personal**
  ([Shared or personal](#shared-or-personal));
- when its last mail came, and **Runs it started →**.

The list has a search box. **New mail trigger**, at the top, is for
project editors and up, and makes a trigger that runs as you: "New mail in
the mailbox starts what you pick, each mail its own run, acting as you.
Mail already there doesn't run: it starts from now." It asks for:

- **Mailbox:** **Gmail** or **Outlook**.
- **Whose:** **Mine, through my own connection**, which you connect first
  on the **Connections** page, or, for project admins, **The company's**
  ([The company's mailbox](#the-companys-mailbox)).
- **Gmail label** or **Outlook folder:** empty for the inbox, else the
  label's id, such as `Label_12`, or the folder's id or well-known name,
  such as `archive`.
- **Starts:** an agent (its pinned version, or the latest), or a flow, at
  its latest version that isn't retired, which the trigger then keeps
  starting. To start another of its versions, create the trigger with the
  CLI's `--flow-version` ([Create one](#create-one)).
- **Looks:** how often it looks for new mail, from every 30 seconds to
  every hour. It's every minute to start with.
- **Runs an hour, at most:** 1 to 600, 60 to start with. Past it, a mail
  never runs, and staff answer it in the mailbox.
- **Label:** its name in lists. Left empty, its id names it.

**Make it** opens the new trigger's page. Its tools allowed unattended
start empty: a project admin lists them with the CLI
([List a tool](#list-a-tool)). A trigger made here on the company's mailbox
is personal to whoever made it. To have the project own it, create it with
`--run-as=project` ([The company's mailbox](#the-companys-mailbox)).

A trigger's page says what it does, such as "New mail in the company's
Gmail, inbox starts the agent Support Desk (its pinned version, or the
latest), one run per mail." Then:

- **Mailbox:** whose mailbox it is, its label or folder, or **Inbox**, the
  account it reads, once it has looked, when it last looked, and its pace,
  such as "Looks every minute, at most 60 runs an hour".
- **Each run:** what a run gets, "The mail and its thread, as its message"
  for an agent, in the thread's own conversation, or "The mail and its
  thread, as fields" for a flow. And **Runs as:** its owner and whether
  it's shared or personal, such as "Sam Lee · Personal", or "This project
  · Shared" when the project's own account owns it.
- **Mail:** a summary, such as "Of its 3 mails, 2 started a run and 1 was
  skipped.", **Runs it started →**, and its latest mails, up to 50: when,
  what happened, and the run it started. These are its
  [fires](#see-what-each-mail-did) in words, such as "Over its
  runs-an-hour limit: this mail never runs", and a mail that never runs
  adds "Answer it in the mailbox." When there are more, the summary says
  "Of the last …", and `kindgi mail-triggers fires` has the older ones.
- **Details,** folded at the foot of the page: its trigger id, its owner,
  the account, its tools allowed unattended, and when it was made.

The mail itself isn't on the trigger's page. It's on each run, for whoever
may read the run.

When a trigger isn't reading mail, its page says why: "The mailbox's
connection is gone or has lapsed. It keeps looking, and reads mail again
once the mailbox is connected again.", with **Reconnect**, or "Its owner
was removed, or may no longer run what it starts. It reads nothing until
that changes or a project admin takes it over." When the runtime paused
it, the page shows the runtime's own sentence.

On the list and on the page:

- **Pause**, **Resume** and **Remove** are for project editors and up on a
  shared trigger, and for its owner and project admins on a personal one.
  Pause: "It stops looking for new mail. When it's resumed it starts from
  then: mail that came while it was paused doesn't run, and staff answer
  it in the mailbox." Remove: "It stops looking for new mail. The runs it
  started stay, with their record of what started them. It can't be
  brought back: a new one starts from when it's made."

On the trigger's page, project admins also get **Take over**: "The runs
its mail starts from now on act as you, with your access." On a person's
mailbox, the trigger then reads your own Gmail or Outlook, from then on,
so you need your own connection to it. Runs already started keep theirs,
and a paused trigger stays paused. On a trigger with tools allowed
unattended, the dialog adds that its runs still call them, with the new
owner's access.

A tenant admin gets **Change owner…** instead, on a trigger on the
company's mailbox, whenever the tenant has a registered service account
the trigger doesn't already run as. Its **Runs as** list has each
registered service account and, when the trigger isn't already yours,
"You — with your access". The dialog says what changes before anything
does, as for
[a schedule](../run-on-a-schedule/#in-the-console). A trigger on a
person's mailbox has only **Take over**: a person's mailbox is read as a
person.

To change what a trigger runs, what it reads or its pace, use
`kindgi mail-triggers update` ([Pause it, change it](#pause-it-change-it)).

A run a mail started says so on its page, under **Started by**: "The mail
trigger “support inbox”", what it watches, whom the run acts for, and
**Its other runs →**, the runs list filtered to that trigger. Once the
trigger is removed, it reads "A mail trigger that's since been removed".

## Turn the mail into fields first

A store that acts on mail can split it in two steps, as a flow:

1. **An agent with no tools** reads the mail and answers fields: what the
   customer wants, the order number, an amount asked for, and a short
   summary.
2. **The agent that acts** gets those fields, not the mail.

The first agent can be steered by the mail, but has nothing to call. The
second reads only fields your flow checked, and its changes still wait for
a person's yes.
