"@kindgi/connections": patch

**A Google or Microsoft connection can be the company's mailbox.** The `google` and `microsoft` entries' `owners` are now `['member', 'company']`: a tenant admin connects the company's mailbox by signing in as that mailbox (`support@acme.example`), not as themselves.
- **Gmail's quota costs** are in the `google` entry's rate limit, in Gmail's own units: reading a message 60 (20 for its headers only), a thread 120, a message list 5, the mailbox's history 2 and its profile 1, beside a send's 100.
- **`gmailMessagesList`** (`@kindgi/connections/google/tools`) lists or searches a Gmail mailbox, newest first: each message's subject, sender, when it came, the start of its text and whether it's unread. It needs only `gmail.readonly`.
- **`as: 'company'`** on `gmailSend`, `gmailMessagesList`, `gmailMessageGet`, `outlookSend`, `outlookMessagesList` and `outlookMessageGet` makes the tool use the company's mailbox instead of the person's own. `as` is part of what a tool declares, so an id you've published keeps its `as`: give a company mailbox its own tool id (`acme.store-mail.list`).
- **`@kindgi/connections/mail`**: `MailMessage`, one shape for a message from Gmail or Outlook, and `MailFunctions`, the calls a runtime makes to watch a mailbox for new mail and read it for a run.
- **A mail's text, from Gmail or Outlook,** keeps where its links go: HTML's link `your order` comes as `your order <https://…>`, unless the text shows the address already. Every web address loses its tracking parameters (`utm_*`, `mc_cid`, `fbclid` and the like). A quoted line (an HTML blockquote's) starts with `> `, one per level, as a mail client quotes. An AMP or watch version of the body isn't an attachment, and an invite is listed once.
- **`gmailMessageGet`** reads one message whole and answers a `MailMessage`: who it's from and to, its reply-to, its subject, its text (the plain-text part, else the HTML as text: the first 50,000 characters), its attachments' names, types and sizes (one the mail shows in its body, a pasted photo say, marked `inline`), and whether it's an automatic reply. It needs only `gmail.readonly`.
- **`googleMail`** (`@kindgi/connections/google/mail`) is Gmail's `MailFunctions`. `start` gives the mailbox's latest history id; `changes` reads the new mail in a label since it; `since` lists a label's mail from a time, for a catch-up after Gmail forgets a history id; and `read` gives a message with its thread, oldest first: the thread's first mail and the newest, within `MAIL_TURN_BUDGET`'s `threadMails`, and how many it didn't take (`notRead`). Each mail's text comes without what it quotes from the thread's earlier mail (an `On … wrote:` line and the `>` lines under it, an Outlook reply's original-message block), marked `[earlier mail left out]`; a forward, an answer typed between quoted lines and a mail that would be left empty keep their text whole. They're called with a token and a `fetch`, and paced by the `google` entry's rate limits, as the tools are.
- **`outlookMessageGet` is at 2.0.0, and answers a `MailMessage`.** This breaks its output: `conversationId` is now `threadId`, and it adds `replyTo`, `attachments` (names, types and sizes, one shown in the body marked `inline`) and `autoSubmitted`. `body` and `bodyCut` stay; a body Outlook gives only as HTML now comes as text too. The `OutlookMessage` type is gone: use `MailMessage` (`@kindgi/connections/mail`, also exported by `@kindgi/connections/microsoft/tools`). An agent that names the tool at `^1.0.0` moves to `^2.0.0`. Without `as`, `gmailSend`, `outlookSend` and `outlookMessagesList` declare what they did before, at the same versions.
- **`microsoftMail`** (`@kindgi/connections/microsoft/mail`) is Outlook's `MailFunctions`. `start` begins a delta of a folder's new mail and keeps its link as the cursor; `changes` reads it; `since` lists a folder's mail from a time; and `read` gives a message with its conversation, oldest first, within the same budget, each mail with only what it added. A message that `changes`, `start` or `since` finds without its conversation comes with it, by a call for that one field. Each call is paced by the `microsoft` entry's rate limits, and none counts against sends. A cursor is only ever a Microsoft Graph link: one changed where it's kept is refused, so the token goes nowhere else.
- **When a mail call fails:** a busy answer is tried again as the tools' calls are (a 429, Google's rate-limit 403s, a 503 to a GET: 3 tries in all). A 401 throws `ConnectionRejectedError`, so the runtime renews the token. Any other answer the result doesn't cover throws `MailCallError`, with the app's status and its reason when that is in a code's form (Gmail's `reason`, Graph's error `code`), never the app's words or the token; `isMailScopeMissing` says when the connection lacks a scope the call needs. A call stopped by its `abortSignal` rejects with the signal's reason.
- When Outlook finds no message, the error now says it found nothing "in the mailbox", which can be the company's.
- **A thread leaves out drafts, trash and spam:** Gmail's `DRAFT`, `TRASH` and `SPAM`, and Outlook's drafts and its Deleted Items and Junk Email. A staff member's unsent reply never reaches a run as sent mail.
- **The agent's own replies:** `MailMessage.sent` is `true` for the mailbox's sent mail in a read's thread (Gmail's `SENT`, Outlook's Sent Items). `replyRefOf(runId)` gives a run's reply mark, which a reply carries in its `X-Reply-Ref` header (`REPLY_REF_HEADER`); `MailMessage.replyRef` gives a Gmail mail's mark when the header is in a mark's form (`isReplyRef`). Anyone can set the header, so a mark counts only on sent mail.
- **`mailTurn`** makes a mail-started turn from a read: the agent's message (the thread as text, oldest first, the new mail last, with who each mail is from and when), a flow target's input (`mail`, `thread`, `leftOut`), and the ids the turn `covered`. Within `MAIL_TURN_BUDGET` (each mail's text 50,000 characters, the turn's 60,000, at most 100 mails), it takes the new mail, then the first mail, then the newest, and says how many mails it left out. On a conversation that continues the thread, it carries only what the conversation wasn't given, and drops the agent's own marked replies. In a turn, a web address still over 100 characters keeps its scheme, host and the start of its path; the read tools keep every address whole.
