When the authorization store can't be reached, a request answers `503 authz-backend-unavailable` with `Retry-After: 5`, not `500 internal-server-error`, where sending it again is safe: a check in the `authorize` middleware, which runs before the handler, on any method, and a check inside a handler on a `GET` or `HEAD`. A check inside a handler on any other method still answers 500, since the handler may have written before it. A route that reads a check as yes or no answers the 503 too, never a 403 or 404 of its own: removing a connection choice, naming a service account as a trigger's owner, closing or unregistering a conversation, and pausing or unregistering an Improve schedule. A check refused before another one allowed the request no longer leaves its `403` on the answer: a project's admin who pauses or unregisters an Improve schedule, and a caller with `read` on its agent alone who reads one, get a `200`. The runtime's authorization binding marks such a failure `code: 'authz-backend-unavailable'`, on the error or down its `cause` chain; a binding that doesn't mark it answers 500, as before. `GET /v1/identity/me/permissions`, which already answered `503 authz-backend-unavailable` when the store couldn't be read, now sends `Retry-After` with it.

The OpenAPI document declares a 503 naming `authz-backend-unavailable` on every operation that declares a 401: the shared `database-busy` and `idempotency-store-unavailable` 503s name it too, the authenticated writes that declared no 503 get one, and the operations with their own 503 add it to theirs.
